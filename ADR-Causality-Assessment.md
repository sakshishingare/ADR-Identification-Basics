# ADR Causality Assessment Report

## Task 3: Causality Assessment of an Adverse Drug Reaction

### 1. Objective
To evaluate the relationship between amoxicillin and the skin rash described in the Task 3 poster case study using the Naranjo Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) Probability Scale.

### 2. Case Summary
The poster describes a 45-year-old female patient who received amoxicillin and developed a skin rash. The reaction appeared three days after the suspected drug was started. The patient recovered after the drug was discontinued.

| Parameter | Case information |
|---|---|
| Patient | 45-year-old female |
| Suspected drug | Amoxicillin |
| Adverse reaction | Skin rash |
| Time to onset | 3 days after starting amoxicillin |
| Outcome | Recovered after discontinuation |

**Details not supplied in the poster:** dose, route, indication, concomitant medicines, relevant medical/allergy history, alternative causes, previous exposure, rechallenge, dose-response information, and detailed clinical examination or laboratory findings. These details have not been assumed.

### 3. Assessment Method: Naranjo ADR Probability Scale
The Naranjo scale consists of 10 questions. Each response is assigned a score, and the scores are added to estimate the probability that the suspected drug caused the adverse reaction.

### 4. Naranjo Assessment and Scoring

| 3 | Did the adverse reaction improve when the drug was discontinued or a specific antagonist was administered? | Yes. The patient recovered after amoxicillin was stopped. | +1 |
| 4 | Did the adverse event reappear when the drug was re-administered? | Not reported. | 0 |

### 5. Score Calculation

**Total Naranjo score = 1 + 2 + 1 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 0 = 4**

### 6. Naranjo Causality Classification

| Total score | Causality category |
|---:|---|
| 9 or more | Definite |
| 5–8 | Probable |
| 1–4 | Possible |
| 0 or less | Doubtful |

**Causality assessment: POSSIBLE (Naranjo score = 4).**

### 7. Interpretation
The temporal relationship supports an association because the skin rash developed three days after amoxicillin was initiated. The patient's recovery after discontinuation of amoxicillin (positive dechallenge) provides additional support. Amoxicillin product information recognises rash as an adverse reaction.

However, the poster does not provide information about alternative causes, previous exposure, rechallenge, dose-response, or objective confirmation of the rash. These missing details limit the strength of the assessment. The available evidence therefore supports a possible relationship, but is insufficient to classify the reaction as probable or definite using the conservative scoring applied here.

### 8. Conclusion
Based on the information available in the Task 3 poster, the suspected amoxicillin-associated skin rash receives a **Naranjo score of 4, corresponding to POSSIBLE causality**. This is an educational assessment based only on the limited case details supplied. A complete clinical assessment would require additional patient history and clinical documentation.
