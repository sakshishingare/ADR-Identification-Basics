## 1. Introduction

Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) reporting is an essential component of pharmacovigilance. It involves the systematic collection, documentation and assessment of suspected adverse reactions associated with medicinal products.

The objective of this exercise is to document a patient case involving suspected amoxicillin-associated skin reactions using a structured ADR reporting format.

## 7. Preliminary Pharmacovigilance Assessment

### Suspected Drug
Amoxicillin 500 mg.

### Suspected Adverse Reaction
Generalized itching and chest skin rash.

### Temporal Relationship
The adverse reaction developed approximately two days after initiation of amoxicillin treatment.

### Dechallenge
Amoxicillin was discontinued on 03 March 2026. Complete recovery was reported on 06 March 2026 following drug withdrawal and antihistamine treatment.

This sequence is compatible with a possible drug-related reaction, although the individual contribution of drug withdrawal and antihistamine treatment cannot be determined.

### Rechallenge
No rechallenge information was reported.

### Preliminary Causality Assessment
The temporal relationship and clinical presentation raise suspicion of an amoxicillin-associated cutaneous adverse reaction. However, a definitive WHO-UMC causality category cannot be assigned confidently because relevant clinical information and alternative-cause assessment are incomplete.

Seriousness cannot be conclusively established from the available case information. No hospitalization, life-threatening event, death or other seriousness criterion was reported. Further information would be required for formal classification.

## 9. Conclusion

This case describes a suspected cutaneous adverse drug reaction following the administration of amoxicillin in a 32-year-old male patient.

The patient developed generalized itching and skin rashes approximately two days after starting treatment. The suspected medicine was discontinued, antihistamines were administered, and complete recovery was reported within three days.
