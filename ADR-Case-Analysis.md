## 1. Introduction

Pharmacovigilance is the science and activities related to the detection, assessment, understanding and prevention of adverse effects or any other drug-related problems.

An Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) is a harmful and unintended response to a medicinal product.

ADR identification is an important activity in pharmacovigilance because early recognition and proper assessment of suspected reactions help improve patient safety and support the safe use of medicines.

## 2. Objectives

- To understand the basic concepts of ADR identification.
- To review patient symptoms and medication history.
- To establish the temporal relationship between drug administration and symptom onset.
- To identify the suspected medicine.
- To assess the possible relationship between the medicine and the reported reaction.

## 3. Patient Case Scenario

A 45-year-old female patient developed rash, itching, nausea and dizziness after starting medication.

### Patient Information

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| Patient | Female |
| Age | 45 years |
| Medical treatment | Amoxicillin and paracetamol |
| Suspected medicine | Amoxicillin |
| Concomitant medicine | Paracetamol (as needed) |

### Presenting Symptoms

1. Rash
2. Itching
3. Nausea
4. Dizziness

## 4. Medication History

The patient had started amoxicillin five days before the assessment. Paracetamol was also being taken as needed.

The available case information does not specify the dose, route, frequency, indication or exact administration dates of paracetamol.

## 5. Timeline Assessment

| Timeline | Event |
|---|---|
| Day -5 | Amoxicillin started |
| Day -2 | Patient developed symptoms |
| Day 0 | Symptoms persisted |

The symptoms appeared approximately three days after starting amoxicillin.

This temporal relationship supports a possible association between amoxicillin exposure and the reported symptoms.

## 6. Identification of Suspected ADRs

| Reported symptom | Possible drug-related effect |
|---|---|
| Rash | Possible cutaneous hypersensitivity reaction |
| Itching | Possible hypersensitivity manifestation |
| Nausea | Possible gastrointestinal adverse effect |
| Dizziness | Possible neurological or nonspecific adverse effect |

Rash and itching occurring together may suggest a hypersensitivity reaction. Nausea and dizziness may represent additional suspected adverse reactions.

However, the available information is insufficient to establish the exact mechanism or confirm drug causality.

## 7. Identification of the Suspected Drug

**Primary suspected drug: Amoxicillin**

### Justification

- Amoxicillin was initiated five days before the assessment.
- The reported symptoms began approximately three days after treatment initiation.
- Rash and itching are recognized adverse reactions associated with amoxicillin.
- Nausea and dizziness are also reported with amoxicillin use.
- The timing provides a possible temporal association.

Paracetamol is a concomitant medication and should also be considered during the assessment. Its exact use relative to symptom onset is not available.

Therefore, amoxicillin is identified as the primary suspected medicine, but causality cannot be confirmed from the information provided.

## 8. Preliminary Causality Assessment

Causality assessment evaluates the possibility that a medicine caused a suspected adverse drug reaction.

Common causality assessment approaches include:

- WHO-UMC causality assessment system.
- Naranjo Adverse Drug Reaction Probability Scale.

For this case, a definitive causality category or Naranjo score cannot be reliably assigned because important information is missing, including:

- Previous history of similar reactions.
- Dechallenge information (whether symptoms improved after stopping the suspected medicine).
- Rechallenge information (whether the reaction recurred after re-administration).
- Alternative medical causes.
- Relevant laboratory and clinical findings.
- Exact medication details.

The case should therefore be documented as a suspected ADR requiring further evaluation.

## 9. Recommended Pharmacovigilance Actions

1. Obtain a complete medication and medical history.
2. Confirm the dose, route and frequency of the suspected medicine.
3. Record the exact onset and progression of each symptom.
4. Evaluate other possible causes and concomitant medicines.
5. Inform the treating healthcare professional promptly.
6. Assess the clinical severity and seriousness of the reaction.
7. Document and report the suspected ADR through the appropriate pharmacovigilance reporting pathway.

If the patient develops breathing difficulty, facial or tongue swelling, fainting or other signs of a severe allergic reaction, immediate emergency medical assessment is required.

## 10. Conclusion

This case demonstrates the basic process of adverse drug reaction identification through patient symptom evaluation, medication history review and timeline assessment.

Amoxicillin is identified as the primary suspected medicine because the symptoms developed after its initiation and include rash, itching, nausea and dizziness.

However, a temporal relationship alone does not establish causality. Further clinical information and appropriate causality assessment are necessary to determine the likelihood of a drug-related reaction.

Early identification, proper documentation and timely reporting of suspected ADRs are essential for improving medication safety and protecting patients.
